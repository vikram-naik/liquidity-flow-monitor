Liquidity Flow Monitor: Methodology

Institutional-Grade Accumulation & Distribution Analysis

1. Core Philosophy: Quantifying Intent vs. Price Action

The standard retail approach to technical analysis relies heavily on historical, lagging price derivatives (moving averages, MACD, RSI) or subjective pattern recognition (trendlines, flags). These methods are flawed because they measure the result of an action rather than the intent behind it.

This dashboard pivots to a professional, institutional-grade methodology: Liquidity Tracking and Order Flow Analysis. It seeks to answer three critical questions:

  • Who is in control? (Measured by the Cumulative Volume Ledger)
  • How urgent is their campaign? (Measured by Ledger Angle/Tilt)
  • When are they initiating the markup? (Measured by the MCS Correlation & Ignition markers)

By anchoring analysis to structural shifts and multiplying "skin-in-the-game" volume (Delivery Quantity) by directional conviction (Money Flow Multiplier), the system isolates institutional accumulation and distribution, stripping out retail noise.

2. The "Day Zero" Anchor (Contextual Volume Profiling)

The "Day Zero" anchor is the foundational edge of the entire system.

  • The Problem with Traditional Indicators: Standard indicators carry infinite historical baggage. A 200-day moving average includes data from regimes that are complete irrelevant to the current market auction.
  • The LFM Edge: By programmatically identifying (or manually setting) a "Day Zero" anchor, the system establishes a precise cost basis for the active institutional campaign. It asks: "Since the smart money stepped in at this structural pivot, what is their net accumulated position?"
  • The Output: This bounded timeframe allows for the calculation of a highly accurate DAVWAP (Anchored Volume Weighted Average Price) and the dvl_cumulative (Ghost Line), aligning the analysis with the real-time physics of the auction.

3. The Cumulative Ledger (Isolating Institutional Flow)

The Cumulative DVL (represented by the Ghost Line) is the core diagnostic tool for identifying hidden accumulation or distribution.

  • Mechanism: It isolates institutional footprints by multiplying Delivery Quantity (actual shares changing hands and being held, not day-trading churn) by the Money Flow Multiplier (where the close occurs relative to the daily range).
  • Application: The power of this ledger is divergence. If the price is grinding sideways in a tight, frustrating range, but the Ghost Line is rising relentlessly at a high angle, it provides mathematical proof of Absorption. It reveals that institutions are aggressively buying every dip without causing a price markup, trapping weak hands who only look at the flat price action.

4. The Role of MCS (Momentum Correlation Score)

Accumulation can last a frustratingly long time. Institutions often build their books slowly to avoid tipping their hand.

  • Mechanism: The MCS measures the 30-day correlation between price and volume.
  • Application: It acts as a "Spark" sensor. It prevents capital from being tied up in an endless accumulation zone (dead money). When a prolonged, quiet accumulation phase (positive Ledger) finally syncs up with price momentum (MCS flipping positive and accelerating), the system identifies that the markup phase is likely imminent.

5. Objective Scoring & Derivative Markers (Coils/Ignitions)

Charting is inherently discretionary and biased. A user's interpretation of a "bull flag" often depends on whether they are already long.

  • Mechanism: The dashboard treats chart signals as mathematical derivatives of the primary liquidity data. It forces potential setups through a rigid 100-point multi-pillar scoring system (Ledger Flow, Momentum, Geometry, Dryness, Proximity to Value).
  • Application: For a "Coil" to trigger, it cannot just be a tight candle. It must exhibit volume dryness, it must be near value (DAVWAP), and the institutional ledger must not be dumping. Boolean filters (e.g., requiring 2-sided wicks) act as strict risk-management gates, ensuring the system prefers missing a marginal setup over presenting a false positive.

Trend Intensity Guide

Legder Tilt (Institutional Conviction)

Measures the strength of cumulative institutional flow. High angles (≥45°) indicate aggressive buying, while lower angles signify slow absorption.

MCS Tilt (Momentum Spark)

Measures acceleration of Correlation. High angles (≥45°) indicate a synchronized momentum spark (baseline = 0.1 shift/day).
